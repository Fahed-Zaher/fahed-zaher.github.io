Case Study

Mars Marine

Gameplay Sound Design & Interactive Audio Implementation

Mars Marine is a gameplay-focused audio project built with Unreal Engine and Wwise. The project explores how sound can respond to player actions, gameplay states, and changing combat conditions.

My work covers custom sound design, recorded and processed voice-over, dynamic mixing, and Blueprint-driven audio implementation.

Overview

Audio Built Around Gameplay

Sound design and implementation working together to support feedback, atmosphere, and player awareness.

Combat Feedback

Weapon audio, surface impacts, and creature sounds reinforce player actions and combat encounters.

Reactive Audio

RTPC-driven heartbeat and whispers, Wwise states, and gameplay-triggered cues respond to changing game conditions.

Dynamic Mixing

Automatic ducking, playback randomization, and voice limiting help manage competing sounds and reduce repetition.

Engine: Unreal Engine 5.8.2
Audio Middleware: Wwise 2025.1
Implementation: Unreal Engine Blueprints

Combat & Creature Audio

Weapon, Impact & Creature Sounds

Audio events connected directly to actions in the game world.

Weapon audio was integrated with gameplay input, allowing the automatic rifle sound to respond to sustained firing. Additional impact sounds provide surface-specific feedback when weapon fire hits rock surfaces.

The alien creature features integrated movement and attack audio, with randomized attack sounds connected to its animation and gameplay logic in Unreal Engine. The focus was on implementing responsive audio events that support the creature's behavior in combat.

Weapon Feedback

Weapon audio responds to player input to reinforce the feel and rhythm of firing.

Surface Impacts

Rock impact sounds reinforce contact between weapon fire and the environment.

Creature Audio

Movement and randomized attack sounds give the alien a more recognizable audio presence.

Reactive Audio

Low-Health Heartbeat & Whispers

Using an RTPC to make audio respond to the player's condition.

A Wwise RTPC controls a low-health audio response, introducing a heartbeat and whispers as the player's health becomes critical. This makes the player's condition audible rather than relying exclusively on visual feedback.

The system connects gameplay data to an evolving audio response, demonstrating how middleware parameters can drive player-facing feedback.

Voice-Over

Character & Commander VO

Recording, processing, and integrating voice into gameplay.

I recorded my own voice for both the player character and the commander, then processed and integrated the recordings into the project.

The commander's post-death voice uses Wwise RTPC volume control and time modulation to shape its playback behavior. This combines voice processing with interactive implementation to support the event in gameplay.

Mixing & Wwise Systems

Managing a Responsive Game Mix

Prioritizing important sounds and keeping repeated events under control.

Automatic ducking across multiple buses helps create space for important audio events. Wwise states and effects shape audio behavior, while voice limiting helps manage how many sound instances can play simultaneously.

Randomization and playback probability settings help reduce predictable repetition. These systems provide greater variation while allowing more control over the behavior of individual sound events.

Automatic Ducking

Bus-level ducking helps competing sounds share space in the mix.

Randomization

Playback variation and probability settings help avoid overly repetitive sound events.

Voice Limiting

Wwise voice management helps control concurrent playback when multiple events overlap.

Unreal Integration

Blueprint-Driven Audio Events

Connecting audio behavior to the flow of gameplay.

Unreal Engine Blueprints were used to trigger and adjust audio behavior around gameplay events. This includes wave-start cues and out-of-bounds feedback, alongside the logic needed to coordinate character, weapon, creature, and other in-game audio.

Integrating Wwise with Blueprints made it possible to iterate on event timing and behavior directly within the game, keeping the audio connected to gameplay rather than treating it as a separate layer.
