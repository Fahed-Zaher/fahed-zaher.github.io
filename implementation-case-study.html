Case Study

Unity × Wwise

Interactive Game Audio Implementation

This project explores the implementation of interactive game audio using Wwise and Unity.

The goal was to build an audio system that responds dynamically to player actions, enemies, environments and gameplay states rather than simply playing isolated sound effects.

Overview

Building Audio Around Gameplay

The implementation was built around the idea that audio should communicate what is happening in the game while also contributing to the atmosphere of the world.

The project combines interactive music, environmental ambience, spatial audio, enemy audio, UI feedback, and gameplay-driven events into a unified Wwise-powered audio system. Rather than treating audio as a collection of individual sounds, the focus was on how those sounds interact with gameplay and respond to the player's actions.

Audio Assets: Some audio assets used in the project were provided with Unity's 3D Game Kit. The project focuses primarily on Wwise implementation, interactive audio systems, spatialization, and gameplay integration.

Unity

Interactive Audio

Interactive Music

Exploration & Combat

Music that responds dynamically to the state of the gameplay.

The music system was built around two primary gameplay states: exploration and combat. Wwise states control the active musical behavior, allowing the soundtrack to transition dynamically as the player's situation changes.

Exploration

The exploration state provides the default musical atmosphere while the player moves through the level.

Combat

When an enemy detects the player, the music switches to the combat state, increasing tension and reflecting the change in gameplay.

Wwise Implementation

The music was organized using a Wwise Switch Container with separate exploration and combat music, while transition rules control how the soundtrack moves between states.

Spatial Audio

Rooms, Portals & Reverb

Creating a world where location affects how sound is perceived.

Spatial audio was implemented using Wwise's environmental audio systems to create distinct acoustic spaces throughout the level.

Rooms

Different areas of the level were defined as separate acoustic spaces, allowing their audio environments to behave differently.

Portals

Portals connect adjacent acoustic spaces and allow audio to transition naturally as the player moves between them.

Reverb

Environmental reverb was used to differentiate spaces and reinforce their physical characteristics.

Attenuation

3D attenuation controls how sounds change in relation to the listener's distance and position.

Wwise Implementation

Wwise Rooms and Portals were configured to define the relationship between the different acoustic spaces within the level.

Environment

Building the Soundscape

Ambient systems designed to make the environment feel alive.

Environmental audio was built from multiple layers rather than relying on a single looping ambience. Different sounds were positioned and controlled to create a layered soundscape throughout the level.

Water

Environmental water sounds were positioned in the world using 3D audio to reinforce nearby water sources.

Ambience

Multiple ambient layers were used to establish the atmosphere and add continuous environmental activity.

Underground

The soundscape changes as the player moves underground, creating a distinct atmosphere from the exterior environment.

Wwise Implementation

Environmental sounds were implemented and positioned using Wwise's ambient audio systems and 3D spatialization.

Gameplay Audio

Audio That Reacts

Sound events connected directly to gameplay behavior.

Enemy Audio

Enemy behaviors trigger audio events that provide feedback during detection, attacks, and combat.

Combat

Combat actions are supported by responsive audio feedback that reinforces the intensity of gameplay.

Surface Audio

Footstep & Landing sounds change according to the surface the player moves across.

Interactions

Interactive objects use Wwise events to provide audio feedback when the player interacts with them.

Enemy Audio

Enemy behaviors were connected to audio events to provide feedback throughout combat.

Dynamic Gameplay Audio

Footstep sounds respond to different surfaces, while interactive objects trigger their own audio events.

UI Audio

Feedback Beyond the World

Audio feedback and music control for menus and player interactions.

UI audio was implemented to provide responsive feedback for menus, player interactions, and pause states. Wwise States were also used to duck the music during menu interactions, while bus volume was mapped to the menu to allow players to control audio levels directly.

Menu Feedback

Navigation and selection sounds provide immediate feedback when interacting with the interface.

Music Ducking

Wwise States were used to automatically duck music during menu and pause interactions with smooth transition.

Audio Control

Bus volume was mapped to the menu, allowing players to adjust the game's audio levels.

Optimization

Efficient Audio Without Sacrificing Detail

Audio systems were structured to manage voice usage, processing, and playback while maintaining a dynamic soundscape.

Voice Limiting

Voice limits were configured to prevent excessive instances of the same sounds from playing simultaneously.

Prioritization

Audio voices were prioritized so important sounds could take precedence when multiple sounds competed for available voices.

Voice Virtualization

Wwise voice virtualization was used to reduce unnecessary CPU processing when sounds were no longer perceptually important.

Probability

Probability settings were used to reduce unnecessary event playback, particularly when multiple enemies were active simultaneously.

Ambient Audio

Large ambient sounds and multi-position playback were used to create environmental coverage while reducing the need for numerous individual audio sources.

Attenuation

Attenuation curves controlled when sounds became inaudible or virtualized based on their distance from the listener.

Sound Variation

Pitch, volume, and filter randomization were used to introduce variation and prevent frequently triggered sounds from becoming repetitive.

Challenges

Problems Became Part of the Process

Building interactive audio also meant solving unexpected technical problems and refining the implementation through iteration.

Music Synchronization

Music transitions initially produced synchronization issues caused by cue markers and transition settings. Adjusting the markers and transition segments resolved the timing problems between music states.

Audio Virtualization

Some looping sounds became virtualized under specific gameplay conditions, requiring investigation of Wwise voice behavior, event structure, and playback conditions.

Spatial Audio & Portals

Rooms and Portals required careful positioning and configuration to maintain consistent spatial behavior. Duplicated or repositioned setups sometimes produced unexpected results.

Gameplay State Integration

Connecting enemy behavior to Wwise states introduced unexpected state transitions, particularly when enemies entered or exited combat. The implementation was refined to keep gameplay and audio states synchronized.

Final Result

From Individual Sounds to an Audio System

The result is a connected interactive audio implementation rather than a collection of isolated sound effects.

The project brings together music, environmental audio, spatial processing, gameplay events, enemy behavior, UI feedback and dynamic parameters into one interactive system.

What I Learned

This project strengthened my understanding of how sound design changes when it becomes part of an interactive system. Designing the sound itself was only one part of the process; implementing it required thinking about gameplay logic, player behavior, spatial relationships and technical limitations.

It also gave me practical experience troubleshooting Wwise integration issues and iterating on systems until the audio behaved reliably inside the game.
